# Capsules, Powders or Gummies: What the Format Actually Changes

**SEO title:** Supplement Capsules vs Powder vs Gummies: What Changes
**Meta description:** What a supplement's format actually changes: serving size, cost per serving and convenience across capsules, powders, gummies, liquids and sachets.
**URL slug:** `supplement-capsules-vs-powder`

---

The format of a supplement, whether it comes as a capsule, tablet, powder, gummy, liquid or sachet, changes several practical things: how you take it, how many units make up a serving, how the cost works out per serving, and how long a pack lasts. What it does not reliably change is a topic for your pharmacist or doctor, and this guide stays firmly on the practical side. If you are deciding between formats, these are the differences that actually affect how the product fits your day and your budget.

## Capsules and tablets: the compact default

Capsules and tablets are the most common format and the easiest to reason about. A serving is usually one or two units, the pack count is stated clearly, and working out servings per container is straightforward. They travel well, need no measuring, and have no taste to speak of, which suits ingredients that would otherwise be unpalatable.

The main practical variable is the serving size. A one-capsule serving and a two-capsule serving change how quickly you get through a pack, which changes cost per serving even when the pack price is identical. Always check the directions rather than assuming one unit equals one serving. Capsules and tablets are also where pack counts vary most, from small packs to large 120-count bottles, so this is the format where per-serving comparison, covered in the [guide to comparing supplements per serving](/articles/supplement-price-per-serving.html), makes the biggest difference to value.

## Powders: flexible dose, more admin

Powders are measured by scoop or weight rather than a fixed unit, which changes a few things. The serving is a stated amount, often a scoop, and the pack is a total weight, so servings per container is the total weight divided by the serving weight. This makes powders slightly more effort to compare, because you are working with grams rather than a tidy unit count.

Powders often work out at a lower cost per serving for the same amount of an ingredient, because you are not paying for the capsule shell or the manufacturing of individual units, and packs tend to hold a lot of servings. The trade-offs are practical: you need to measure, you need water or a drink to mix into, they are less portable, and taste and mixability become factors. For someone who takes a supplement at home each day, a powder can be efficient. For someone who needs to take it on the go, less so.

## Gummies: the palatable format, with things to check

Gummies exist mainly to make taking a supplement pleasant, and that is a genuine benefit for people who dislike swallowing capsules. The practical points to check are on the label. The serving is usually two or more gummies, so the pack count divided by the serving size gives you fewer servings than the gummy count suggests. Gummies also contain the ingredients that make them gummies, including sweeteners or sugars, which are listed in the ingredients and worth reading if that matters to you.

On value, gummies often carry a higher cost per serving than capsules or powders delivering a similar amount, because the format itself costs more to make. That is not a criticism, it is a trade you may happily make for a format you will actually take consistently. A supplement you enjoy taking and therefore take is more useful to you than one you avoid, and that is a legitimate reason to choose gummies even at a higher per-serving cost.

## Liquids and sachets: pre-measured convenience

Liquids and single-serving sachets pre-measure the dose for you, which removes the admin of powders while keeping some of their flexibility. Sachets in particular are portable and unambiguous, one sachet is one serving, which makes servings per container simply the sachet count. Many collagen products come in this format, and you can see examples on the [supplements category page](/supplements). The convenience often comes at a higher cost per serving than a bulk powder, again the standard trade of convenience against unit cost.

## How to choose the format for you

The sensible way to choose is to hold two things in mind at once: which format you will realistically use consistently, and what it costs per serving. These sometimes point in the same direction and sometimes do not. A powder might be best value per serving but a capsule might be the one you will actually remember to take at your desk. There is no universally correct format, only the one that fits your routine and your budget.

Whichever you choose, compare on a per-serving basis rather than by pack price or unit count, because as the [per-serving guide](/articles/supplement-price-per-serving.html) explains, unit counts mean different things in different formats and the sticker price hides the real value. Reading the label properly, covered in [how to read a supplement label](/articles/how-to-read-supplement-label.html), gives you the serving size and servings per container you need to do that.
